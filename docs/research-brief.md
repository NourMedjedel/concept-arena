# ConceptArena: beauty research and product direction

Research date: 19 November 2026. Source links rechecked on the same date.

## Recommendation

Position the demo as a beauty concept decision studio: connect what participants
noticed, understood, wanted and questioned to a specific design decision and its
supporting evidence. Editable slides are an important deliverable, but no longer
a sufficient differentiator by themselves.

The distinctive loop should be:

**Pack detail → participant response → interpreted finding → proposed change →
next research question → client-ready slide.**

Keep the fictional identity and original product renders. Do not identify former
clients, imply a partnership, or include proprietary research in this project.

## What this research establishes

Sources include public beauty-group announcements, an annual report, research
agency descriptions, research-platform documentation and public report summaries.
They establish capabilities and stated priorities, not a private audit of any
company's workflow. Proposed unmet needs below are product hypotheses to test.
Paid report tables and private company materials were not accessed.

## How the work connects

The following is a proposed workflow informed by those public sources and the
user's experience. It is not a claim that every beauty firm follows one process.

| Stage | Research question | Useful software behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief and stimuli | What decision is the study meant to inform? | Record concept version, intended message, price, exposure and research questions. |
| Concept/pack exposure | What do participants notice and understand? | Keep pack-only reactions separate from responses after reading the concept. |
| Individual interpretation | What does this person actually say across questions? | Preserve stated choices, reasons, reservations and changes of context. |
| Cross-case synthesis | Which patterns recur, and where do they break? | Deduplicate participants, inspect counterexamples and keep missing answers visible. |
| Decision and revision | What should design, product and marketing do next? | Link a proposed change to its evidence and identify what still needs testing. |
| Readout and reuse | Can others inspect and reuse the result? | Export editable slides, analysis workbooks and an evidence trail. |

## Current needs worth demonstrating

### 1. Separate desirability, comprehension and choice

A participant can admire the packaging, misunderstand its refill mechanism and
still choose another concept for personal use. Represent these as different
observations, each linked to its question and source excerpt.

The first study should compare gifting and personal purchase only where those
questions were explicitly asked. Use counts and denominators for the included
participants. Twelve qualitative interviews do not establish a population winner.

### 2. Connect language to the thing being designed

Give each fictional pack a small set of named regions, such as cap, body, label
and refill interface. A participant quotation about the cap can open that region
in the product view. These are semantic annotations, not measured visual attention.

The model may propose a region and theme. Ambiguous mappings remain unassigned
until reviewed. Never manufacture a quote to explain a visual feature.

### 3. Make researcher corrections operational

When a researcher changes a code, show the affected participant counts, findings
and slides. Recalculate quantities deterministically and mark dependent narrative
for review. Do not silently publish a changed interpretation.

When the pack itself is revised, create a new stimulus version. Existing feedback
belongs to the version people saw. Present the new design as untested and create
a retest question rather than predicting that respondents will prefer it.

### 4. Deliver something the client can continue working with

Generate editable slide objects, quote cards and charts, accompanied by source
references and an evidence appendix. Include an XLSX analysis export containing
the participant matrix, reviewed codes, findings and source coordinates.

## Near-future signals, with uncertainty

**Connected decision workflows.** The direction announced in [S6] suggests demand
for tools that integrate into existing product processes. An API-ready evidence
model and versioned handoffs are a sensible foundation. This is an inference from
announced priorities, not proof of an unfilled procurement need.

**AI-mediated beauty discovery.** A September 2026 announcement addresses how
products appear in AI recommendations [S9]. A future module could compare approved
product facts with generated product descriptions or assistant answers. It should
test factual preservation, not claim to predict or manipulate recommendation rank.

**Routine and occasion relevance.** A November 2026 industry forecast for 2027
emphasises consumer outcomes and connected routines [S10]. The public US colour
cosmetics summary also discusses distinct value and occasion patterns [S11]. These
are reasons to preserve use context, not to assume that a US trend applies to all
markets or that a forecast is an observed future outcome.

**Human and synthetic evidence.** Ipsos guidance stresses that useful synthetic
data depends on real human data, research expertise and validation [S12]. Do not make simulated shoppers the source
of a supposed market winner. The demo's authored dataset tests software behaviour;
it must remain labelled synthetic in the app and in exports.

## What to build first

One study, three fictional premium lip-colour packs, twelve fictional participants,
French/English source text, personal/gifting context and recorded exposure phase.
Keep formula, shade, size, refill function and stated price constant. Vary pack
design and the way it communicates the same facts.

The first release should include only three headline interactions:

1. **Inspect:** choose a pack detail, then read the linked individual responses,
   relevant findings and counterexamples.
2. **Compare:** switch the purchase occasion and inspect the corresponding stated
   choices and reasons. Display missing answers and denominators.
3. **Revise:** correct an interpretation, review the impact on findings/slides,
   approve the revised narrative, and export the updated report.

A small revision panel can show one pre-authored proposed pack version and a
retest question. Live image generation, eye tracking, simulated populations,
predicted sales and an all-in-one research repository are outside the first demo.

## Technical work that gives this substance

- Validated XLSX ingestion into stable participant, stimulus, question, context,
  response and source-coordinate records.
- NLP proposals for themes, reasons, reservations and explicit choices, with
  quoted spans and a reviewed/ambiguous state. Preserve original language.
- An evidence/dependency graph linking pack regions, responses, coding decisions,
  findings and slide objects. Keep model proposals separate from accepted data.
- Deterministic quantities and dependency invalidation after corrections.
- Editable PPTX/XLSX generation from approved structured findings.
- A separately labelled evaluation set for negation, mixed opinions, context
  changes, participant deduplication and French/English variation.

Use real model processing for proposed analysis. A free public sample may use
clearly identified cached analysis; record its model/configuration and do not
represent playback as live inference.

## Evidence of a successful prototype

- A visitor can complete the sample story without signup or a paid API.
- Every displayed finding opens the exact source material that supports it.
- A repeated speaker cannot inflate the participant count by making more comments.
- A correction has a visible, reproducible effect on the evidence and report.
- Untested design revisions remain distinct from tested stimuli.
- The downloaded deck contains editable content and matches approved findings.
- A short methods note distinguishes synthetic demo data, measured quantities,
  interpretations and remaining research questions.
